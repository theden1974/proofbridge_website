## Evidence: From the field.
### Elderly care
- Starting point: A funding of 1 million euros, to help long-term unemployed people work in the care of the elderly. They already decided they probably needed a mobile phone app.
- What I saw: I asked if I could interview the people they already deployed and the elderly still living independently at home. The elderly were indeed not good at mobiles and apps. They wanted to be self sufficient.
- After: I created a concept called zorg concierge: no app for the elderly, only an app for the employee working in the flat. It was not built out. They scaled the elderly homes solution instead.

### Healthcare
- Starting point: The task was optimizing app screens for dietitians.
- What I saw: It was not so much the dietitian that needed the right app or platform to enter data, but making the patient the center of the concept.
- After: My follow up question: how do you know if your AI models are based on the right data (entries)? A patient weight over time has so many input points: doctor, at home, nurse, morning, afternoon.

### Water authority
- What I saw: As far as I have seen or understood, the permit officer is there to fill a system with data. Ask what happens with a data point and it is not about the purpose of the data point but the place it goes next: department x, y, z.
- After: The purpose of the data point, how it can be applied, when, why and in what capacity, is not clear. (Translated from Dutch.)

### Legal, architecture
- Starting point: An architect used AI to summarise a 60 page legal document. The output seemed legit.
- What I saw: He checked the output against the referenced paragraphs and they were off.
- After: When brought to a legal case it wouldn't hold.

### Building with AI
- What I saw: The failure that keeps coming back is my inability to interface with the chat in such a way that I can draw the outline of what I expect it to do and how I expect the system to work. There are constant misalignments. And I noticed the lock-in of the different models, and the direction it is heading.
- After: As for now I just have been feeding the system and building the system to understand what it is capable of.

### Method
- Real in life experiencing: seeing the actual product at work, seeing the problems and spotting the underlying issues while using the product, the platform or seeing the customer interaction.
- Interviews and the Kano model, to understand the must haves compared to the nice to haves.
- There is always a translation between the stakeholder that thinks what is needed and the actual user that can express or audit what is needed.
- The output is audited by the actual stakeholder for whom the problem occurs. I just translate.
