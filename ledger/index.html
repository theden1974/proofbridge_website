IT GIVES A DIFFERENT ANSWER EVERY DAY. WHO IS DOING THE THINKING?

Rick Rubin is talking with Steven Bartlett about making things, and they have come to AI and whether it can be creative, just after he has said that when he calls something good he means good to him.

AI doesn't have a point of view AI is the collected ideas that already existRick Rubin, The Diary of a CEO

What I hear under this line is where the answer comes from. It is collected ideas, theory gathered and held on the internet, and the reasoning runs on top of that without a view of its own. That is why it can say something different tomorrow. The danger I see is the loop: once experts start generating their expertise with AI, the AI reuses that output again and eventually feeds on itself. The creativity has not left, it has moved to the person who asks, the way Warhol did not put the paint on the canvas himself. So when something is presented to me as the machine thinking, I look under it, ask what is driving the answer, and name that instead. That is the move I make in every field, long before AI.

↳ what surrounds the tool

SAME MODEL, TWO PRODUCTS. ONE OF THEM HURTS TO USE.

Nate B Jones is telling his viewers to stop reading AI leaderboards, because the AI that wins is built by people who use it all day and fix what breaks, and then he places where the pain comes from.

the pain points it's not coming from the models it's coming from the product experienceNate B Jones, On AI leaderboards

Intelligence and reasoning on their own are purposeless to me. It is what I always see with new laptops: a faster CPU, faster GPU, so many times faster than the previous generation, and normal people won't see measurable changes in their use. Here the same thing is said about AI. The leaderboard compares the brains inside, while what hurts in use comes from the product wrapped around them. Every AI chat is exactly the same, no interface that looks awkwardly different but works much, much better. Anything is an experience, and how the interaction is experienced is where the difference is made. So I stop looking at the model and look at what surrounds it.

↳ checkable at a glance

WHY SHOULD I LISTEN TO YOU? SHOW ME SOMETHING I CAN CHECK.

Naval Ravikant, answering questions after the main episodes, is on how a founder shows what they are worth, and he borrows a word from crypto for it: proof of work.

the product progress is the resume for the entrepreneurNaval Ravikant, How to Get Rich

A resume is a claim I have to believe; progress on a product is something I can look at. That difference is the whole thing for me. I am like AI in that I always sound reasonable and trustworthy, but it is all about the execution of the insights, and that is what has to be visible. What I trusted in a builder I worked with was the way he laid his expertise side by side with things I already knew, so I could check him without having his knowledge. Don't pretend you know it is working, know it is working: build it, use it, break it, learn from the failure. So a claim has to be laid out in such a way that anyone can see for themselves what is behind it, without first becoming an expert. That is what I mean when I say proof of fact.

↳ only an expert can audit it

THREE ANSWERS, ALL DIFFERENT. SOMEONE STILL HAS TO HEAR WHICH IS OFF.

Rick Rubin is describing what he does with an artist in the studio: he asks them to play him their favourites, and finds they cannot hear themselves.

I could hear what it could beRick Rubin, The Diary of a CEO

The artist cannot hear his own work; the producer hears in one listen what it could be. That is knowledge from doing, the difference between someone fresh from school with theory and no hands on, and an experienced expert. It is by experience and the repetition of that experience, with the successes and the failures, that it eventually becomes a path you walk without thinking. My guess is that AI can manufacture reasonable output for somebody without the field knowledge, who figures it is good enough, and the senior with years of experience catches the faults. The value lies in the one that breaks it and is also capable of reshaping it from an expert perspective, never from ego or an assumption. Not a long test and not a metric: one hearing by someone with the ear for it. That is why the catch stays with the expert, whatever made the work.

Somebody has to determine that this slop is too sloppyAlex Hormozi, The Diary of a CEO
